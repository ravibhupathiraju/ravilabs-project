How this works — the question being answered, the conditions tested, and the honesty checks

The question: from a given moment in the day, will the price touch current + $target before current − $target (a long day-trade wins) or the reverse (a short wins) — any time before the same session's close? Every 15/30/60 minutes from 10:00 to 14:30 ET is treated as such a decision point, and each one is labeled with what actually happened next (first barrier touched, or neither by the close; when both barriers fall inside one 5-minute bar it counts as a win for neither side — conservative).

The conditions (all measurable at that moment, no look-ahead): tod time of day · gap overnight gap vs prior close (±0.1/0.3%) · or30 above/inside/below the first-30-min range · vwap_side above/below session VWAP (well = >0.15% away) · vwap_slope VWAP direction over the last 30 min · rsi intraday RSI(14) bucket · pd_pos position vs prior day's high/low/midpoint · dow day of week · first30 first-half-hour direction · yday prior day up or down · range_used today's range so far vs the 14-day average daily range · mom30 last-30-min momentum.

The mining: every single condition, pair, and triple is tested. Days are split ~70/30 into an older train and a recent test period, and a pattern is ranked by its worst half — it must beat the baseline in both periods to appear at all. A more complex pattern is dropped when a simpler one inside it scores about as well.

Excluded days: FOMC decision days (2023–2026 built in), NFP first-Fridays, half sessions, plus anything you add to event_days.json (paste CPI dates there — they are not built in). Caveats: decision points within one day overlap, so judge a pattern by its Days column, not just samples; and with ~300 combos tested, some look good by luck — the train/test filter reduces this but cannot eliminate it. A pattern is a starting hypothesis to forward-test, not a guarantee. Educational use only; not financial advice.

What this checks — the "divergences never lie" setup, drawn on your chart

The classic swing-analyst play: after a decline from a swing high (HH) through a lower high (LH) to a low (LL), price prints a lower low while RSI prints a higher low — bullish divergence, momentum turning up before price. The bounce is then measured with a Fibonacci retracement of the LL→LH swing: EQ (50%) and the golden pocket (0.618–0.786) are the decision zones, with LH and HH the targets above. This tab detects the structure automatically on the daily chart, checks the divergence (regular and hidden), confirms with MACD, then draws every level, the golden-pocket band, the swing markers and the divergence line — and tells you plainly whether the setup exists, is confirmed, or has failed. The bearish mirror is detected when the recent extreme is a high. Educational use only; not financial advice.

Structure & levels

The read

    📚 Coach me: divergences on the smaller vs bigger picture

    The one idea: a divergence is a disagreement between price and momentum (RSI). Price makes a new low, but RSI makes a higher low — sellers pushed price lower but with less force. That loss of force shows up first on the smaller (lower) timeframe, because a lower timeframe reacts to momentum sooner. You then read it on the bigger (higher) timeframe to decide whether it actually matters.

    Smaller picture (4H / 15M) = the trigger — WHEN. This is where a divergence prints early. It is sensitive, so it fires often — most of those are noise.

    Bigger picture (Daily) = the context — WHERE. The daily sets the dominant trend and the key levels (the swing structure, EQ, the golden pocket). It answers: is this a spot where a turn should happen?

    The rule that separates signal from noise: only trust a smaller-picture divergence when it agrees with the bigger picture — same direction as the higher-timeframe setup, and ideally firing at a bigger-picture level (EQ or the golden pocket). A smaller-picture bullish divergence inside a bigger-picture uptrend, right at support, is the high-conviction "stack." The same divergence against the bigger trend, in no-man's-land, is just a pullback — ignore it.

    Two flavors (this tab labels which): regular divergence warns of a reversal (price lower low, RSI higher low → down-move exhausting); hidden divergence signals continuation (price higher low, RSI lower low → trend resuming after a dip). Hidden divergences are the ones that "agree with the bigger picture" by construction.

    How to use this tab to learn it: read the Timeframe stack box first — it tells you whether the pictures agree. Then flip the chart between Daily → 4H → 15M and watch how the same golden-pocket price band (drawn on every timeframe) lines up with where each timeframe's cyan divergence line sits. When all three point the same way at that band, that is the setup. When they fight, sit out.
